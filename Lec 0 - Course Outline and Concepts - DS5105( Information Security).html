Course Overview

Information security is concerned with protecting information, information systems, people, devices, and organizational resources from unauthorized access, misuse, modification, disruption, destruction, and other security threats.

“How can we make sure that the system, its data, and its users remain secure?”

Security should not be treated as something added after a system is developed. It needs to be considered throughout the life of an information system.

Security failures can lead to: financial loss, privacy violations, service disruption, data manipulation, identity theft, reputational damage, and legal consequences.

The CIA Triad

Three fundamental security properties provide a foundation for thinking about information security.

🔒

Confidentiality

Information should only be accessible to authorized individuals or systems.

Example: A student's examination results should not be accessible to another student.
✓

Integrity

Information should remain accurate, complete, and trustworthy.

Example: An unauthorized person should not be able to change a student's grade.
⚡

Availability

Information and services should be available when authorized users need them.

Example: An online banking system should remain accessible when customers need it.

What You Will Develop

Security Foundations

Understand fundamental security concepts and principles.

Security Mechanisms

Understand mechanisms such as cryptography, steganography, authentication, authorization, and access control.

Information Assurance

Understand threats, vulnerabilities, attacks, and countermeasures.

Security Operations

Explore auditing, asset management, standards, cost-benefit considerations, and legal issues.

Security Attacks

Understand social engineering, denial of service, protocol attacks, malware, and other attacks.

Digital Forensics

Understand digital evidence, rules of evidence, search and seizure, and media analysis.

0x01 Framing

What are we actually protecting?

"Security" is not one property — it's a bundle of guarantees a system makes about information and access. Computer & network security exists because Alice and Bob are not alone on the wire: Trudy is always a possible participant. Before we can define what is broken when Trudy succeeds, we need the vocabulary security professionals use to talk about how and how badly it can be broken. That's section 0x02.

Goal propertiesCIA + authenticity, non-repudiation, accountability — what we protect.

0x04 Beyond CIA

Accountability, Non-repudiation & Authenticity

CIA doesn't answer "who did this, and can they deny it?" These three properties close that gap — they're what let a security incident end in a courtroom instead of a shrug. Click a card to expand.

01＋

Authenticity

The guarantee that data, a message, or an identity really is what/who it claims to be.

Definition: Authenticity verifies the origin of information — that a message from "Alice" was actually created by Alice, and a server claiming to be "bob-bank.com" really is.

Scenario: Trudy sends Bob an email that looks like it's from Alice ("Bob, wire the funds to this account"). Without an authenticity check (e.g. a digital signature or a verified certificate), Bob has no way to know Alice never sent it. This is the vulnerability behind phishing and email spoofing.

Typical controls: digital signatures, MACs, TLS certificates, mutual authentication, DKIM/SPF/DMARC for email.

02＋

Non-repudiation

Alice cannot later deny she sent the message; Bob cannot deny he received it.

Definition: Provides undeniable proof of origin (and optionally receipt), so a party can't successfully disown an action after the fact. It depends on authenticity plus a durable, tamper-evident record.

Scenario: Alice digitally signs a purchase order to Bob's store. Later she tries to claim "I never ordered that." Because the signature was produced with Alice's private key — something only she holds — Bob (and a third party / judge) can cryptographically prove otherwise.

Typical controls: digital signatures + trusted timestamps, audit logs, certified delivery receipts.

03＋

Accountability

Every action can be traced back to the entity that performed it.

Definition: Accountability = identification + logging + auditing, tying actions to accounts so misuse can be investigated after the fact. It's what turns a security control into a deterrent.

Scenario: Trudy compromises a low-privilege account and deletes a file. Because the system logged who / what / when / from where for every action, the audit trail lets the security team reconstruct exactly what Trudy did — even though she was never caught in the act.

Typical controls: audit logging, SIEM, unique user IDs (no shared logins), tamper-evident logs, video/badge logs for physical access.

0x05 Access Control Model

Authentication → Authorization → Accounting (AAA)

Before Bob's system trusts anything Alice does, it runs her through this pipeline. Click each stage to see what question it answers and to step through a live login console.

STEP 1

Identification

Alice claims an identity: "I am alice@corp".

STEP 2

Authentication

Alice proves it — password, OTP token, fingerprint.

Q: can you prove that?

STEP 3

Authorization

The system checks Alice's permissions against the resource she's requesting.

Q: are you allowed to do this?
›
STEP 4

Accounting

The action, timestamp, and outcome are logged — feeding Accountability.

Q: what did you just do?

Note the distinction: authentication asks "who are you, prove it," while authorization asks "now that I know who you are, what are you allowed to touch." Trudy can pass step 2 (e.g. with a stolen password) and still fail step 3 if she lacks the right role.

0x06 Operational Framework

Identity & Access Management (IAM)

IAM is the organizational system — policies, processes, and tooling — that makes AAA work at scale across an entire company, not just one login. It governs Alice's entire lifecycle as a digital identity, from the day she's hired to the day she leaves. Click a stage on the ring.

Least privilege, enforcedIAM makes "only what's needed, nothing more" an operational default rather than a hope.
Single source of truthOne identity system (often with SSO) instead of dozens of disconnected logins Trudy could target.
Fast, complete offboardingWhen Alice leaves, IAM revokes every system at once — no orphaned accounts left for Trudy to find later.
